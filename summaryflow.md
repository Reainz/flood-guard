FloodGuard — Core Feature
"Know Your Risk Before the Water Rises"
FloodGuard watches two things at the same time — your crops and the weather. When a flood is coming, it brings both together and tells you exactly what to do before it is too late.

How It Works
The farmer registers once.
They enter their farm location, crop type, and current growing season. That is all. FloodGuard takes care of everything else automatically.

Step 1 — We predict your harvest
FloodGuard uses satellite images of the farmer's field taken from space, combined with local weather data like temperature and rainfall, to estimate how much crop that farm is expected to produce this season. This prediction is an AI model that has been trained to read crop health from satellite imagery and weather patterns. The result is a simple number — the farmer's expected harvest this season — which represents what they stand to lose if a flood hits.

Step 2 — We watch for floods
At the same time, FloodGuard monitors the rivers and rainfall near the farmer's field every single day, a global flood monitoring system used by governments and disaster agencies worldwide. When river levels start rising dangerously, FloodGuard detects it up to 72 hours before the flood reaches the farm.

Step 3 — FloodGuard Precision Risk Analysis
The Logic:
Loss = Potential Yield × % Yield Loss (from Lookup Table)
When a flood is detected, FloodGuard uses  observation approaches(satellite imagery, IOT, Computer vision...) to instantly identify the status of crops and other external factors. It then crosses this with the predicted flood's depth and duration using a multivariable lookup table.

1. The Calculation
FloodGuard calculates the specific yield loss based on scientific vulnerability curves (lookup tables) by integrating three critical variables: the rice growth stage, the inundation level (depth), and the inundation duration (days). This multivariable function identifies that rice crops are generally most vulnerable during the ripening stage and most robust during the reproductive stage 

2. Actionable Smart Alerts
Instead of a simple "yes/no" on harvesting, the alerts are now prioritized by the calculated Yield Loss Percentage:
Ripening Stage: The system recognizes this as a high-risk phase where complete inundation lasting 5–8 days causes yield loss to jump dramatically from 50% to 90%.
Reproductive Stage: The model categorizes this as the most resilient phase; even 7 days of 75% inundation results in hardly any yield decrease, while complete flooding for the same period reduces yield by almost half.
Vegetation Stage: The system accounts for moderate vulnerability, noting that 4–5 days of complete flooding during this stage causes nearly the same damage as 7 days of flooding during the more robust reproductive stage.



Step 4 — We help after the flood too
Once the flood passes, farmers can report their losses directly through the app. These reports are automatically collected and sent to local agricultural authorities and relief organizations — so seeds, fertilizer, and recovery support reach the right farmers faster.

Why This Matters
Today, Vietnamese farmers receive generic weather warnings that tell them a storm is coming — but nothing about what it means for their specific farm or what they should do about it. FloodGuard changes that. For the first time, a farmer in the Mekong Delta or the Red River Delta can know exactly what their harvest is worth this season, exactly when a flood is threatening it, and exactly what action to take — all from their phone, in their own language, days before the damage is done.
